# Evidence Atlas scroll design

**Selected direction** · Evidence Atlas, refined after the CropEvidence reference  
**Website story** · Stadium → feed → replay window → signed requirement → reviewer decision → sponsor evidence

The homepage begins with the scale of a stadium and ends with a specific decision a reporting lead can defend. At each transition, the same event gains a new layer of meaning. The venue map becomes a feed diagram. The feed diagram becomes two comparable sources. The source comparison becomes a case with an owner, a decision and an export. Reserve significant screen space for the copy and artifact in each scene. Keep the intricate details inside the map, the clip and the report.

## What to take from CropEvidence

CropEvidence introduces one field, then reveals supporting evidence layers, a source conflict, distinct product cards, a human review gate and a follow-up packet. The public homepage copy, archived browser captures and local motion source show how a familiar subject changes as the reader learns more. The supporting sections also change canvas, illustration format and visual density. Use this pacing for the SponsorEvidence site. Its recurring subject is a stadium replay, rendered through flat maps, graphic traces, photographs and original product UI. [CropEvidence homepage](https://cropevidence.com/)

The [previous visual research](SponsorEvidence-Visual-Directions-and-Motion.md) remains the component reference for pricing, footers, CTA treatment, maps, tables and dashboard layouts. This document refines Evidence Atlas and its motion. The [website structure and copy](SponsorEvidence-Website-IA-and-Copy.md) and the [Placement Audit handoff](SponsorEvidence-Placement-Audit-Handoff.md) supply the page content and source of truth for cases.

## The first 3 scroll beats

**First screen.** Headline and actions render together on the left. On the right, the stadium opens from a top-down plan to a detailed venue illustration with nested service, bowl and pitch outlines. A route legend under the artwork names Main broadcast, Vertical feed and Venue feed. An inset filmstrip contains three consecutive stills from the same fictional replay. Keep the first filmstrip frame visible before the first scroll.

**First scroll.** Pin the map briefly on wide screens. A fine cobalt line draws from the selected venue marker to the main broadcast card. A small source drawer reveals format, source ID and date. As the reader continues, the map adds the official vertical feed and its 9:16 crop guide. The small screen has the same three routes in a list next to a simplified diagram.

**Second scroll.** The route becomes a horizontal shared time ruler at 00:18:42. The 16:9 still reveals from the left and the 9:16 still from the right. A label points to the Northline mark in the main broadcast. The matching safe area in the required vertical feed is empty. The clause enters from above and the chapter ends on the words “The agreement calls for the vertical opening replay.”

## Motion and responsive behavior

- Use Lenis on marketing routes and one GSAP ticker. ScrollTrigger owns progress, masks and intervals. At a proposed minimum of 1200 px wide and 800 px high, use a bounded hero transition of at most about 120vh of extra scroll and the four-product track. Let scroll proceed naturally as each pin releases. Direct links serve readers who want to skip the animation.
- Animate the relationship between objects. Left and right bring source views together, upward motion introduces headings and evidence plates, and downward movement extracts clause and provenance details. Keep entrance distances around 18–32 px for text and 24–64 px for major objects. Start with 420–650 ms entrances and restrained 45–75 ms staggering. Use flatter, faster cuts for the media sequence.
- When the four-product index advances, preserve focus and update the active panel label. Use the actual rendered width of the track for travel and recompute after fonts and image layout settle. Provide previous, next and skip links and show the same four cards on smaller screens.
- At 390 px, keep the hero map as a readable simplified image with a route legend. Disable pins, stack visuals and use one directly controlled feed switch plus a shared time marker in the demo. At reduced motion, show final stills in DOM order and switch immediately between selected states.
- The CTA, labels, headings and case controls remain available in initial HTML. Scroll never records a review decision, changes permission, or submits a form. A keyboard user can operate every interactive source and navigate away from any pin. Add a motion pause control and ensure lazy-loaded visuals do not shift layout.
- Use a single sequence of licensed or original fictional source imagery across the story. The 16:9 and 9:16 views must show the same moment. Keep the Northline mark in a real replay graphic rather than attaching a fake box to the stadium. Use original plotted map art, separate editable route geometry and status overlays that remain readable after export. ## Build order and acceptance

1. Produce the wordmark, original stadium map, paired fictional stills and replay graphic. Establish the ledger grid, colors and typography.
2. Build the hero, map layers and first live handoff into the comparison. Verify there is new, readable information as the visitor advances through each stop. Confirm direct access to the demo and pilot at initial load.
3. Connect the product track and the two-case workbench. Verify the confirmed discrepancy, unresolved source gap, reviewer actions and fictional export at 1440 px and 390 px.
4. Finish all brochure and utility chapters, then extend the existing multi-page IA. Test keyboard, reduced motion, touch, loaded and unloaded media, mobile layout and animation cleanup after navigation.

The vertical feed has complete coverage for that window, with the expected overlay empty. Keep the workbench’s own decision panel in its initial state until the visitor acts.
