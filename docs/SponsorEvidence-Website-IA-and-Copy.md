# SponsorEvidence website structure and copy

**Positioning** · Sports sponsorship delivery verification for rights holders and measurement agencies  
**Primary buyer** · Head of Partnership Reporting  
**Primary conversion** · Request a paid Placement Audit pilot  
**Secondary conversion** · Explore the fictional Placement Audit demo  
**Stage** · Concept preview and paid evaluation design

SponsorEvidence helps rights holders check whether agreed sponsor placements appeared in the feeds and events they sold. The proposed workflow connects approved artwork and licensed footage to a source-backed decision a reviewer can inspect. Placement Audit is the first paid offer.

Use **SponsorEvidence** everywhere a visitor sees the brand. The pasted draft uses SponsorFacet as a legacy working name and mentions sponsorfacet.com. Treat the new name and any candidate domain as pending clearance. The $100,000 ambition concerns the quality of the website and platform experience. The commercial copy uses the brief’s separate $42,000 annual software license hypothesis. The referenced uploaded Markdown and video were unavailable for this pass, so this draft uses the pasted brief. Reconcile against the Markdown first if it becomes available.

**Header.** Wordmark → Platform → Use cases → Resources → Pricing → Sign in → Explore demo → Request a paid pilot. The Platform menu starts with the overview, then shows the four modules in workflow order. Placement Audit gets a visible “First paid evaluation” label. Use cases links to the three buyer pages. Resources contains Methodology, Data & permissions and Sources. Keep both conversion paths visible in the mobile drawer. The mobile header shows the wordmark, menu and demo shortcut.

**Conversion paths.** An analyst opens the fictional demo, inspects the clause and feeds, records a local decision and exports the example. A budget owner reads scope and pricing, requests a paid evaluation, confirms the buyer and data access, agrees on a baseline and signed scope, runs the comparison, then reviews the result with the named reviewer and buyer. Agencies and production partners enter through their use case or Contact. Footer links provide a second way to reach every page family.

### The buyer problem

**H2** · “One replay. Two different sponsor outcomes.”

“A logo can be clear on the main broadcast and missing from the version a sponsor paid to appear on. The exposure dashboard tells you where visibility was measured. The placement schedule tells you what was promised. The reviewer still needs to match the right clip to the right requirement.”

- “Analysts need the source and timecode behind a flag.”
- “Commercial teams need an item they can take into a make-good discussion.”

**Visual.** Warm paper background. The wide still stays fixed while a crop boundary moves across an empty vertical safe area. The diagram labels “promised feed” and “observed feed” separately.

### The solution and AI workflow

**H2** · “Trace a finding back to the recorded feed.”

“Start with approved artwork, authorized recordings and a versioned schedule. Computer vision proposes where an approved mark appears and estimates its duration, position and visible portion. **Visual.** An evidence line links a mark swatch, clip frame, schedule clause and record. Each stage reveals its input and output as it enters the viewport.

## 3. Platform and individual product pages

### Platform overview · /platform

**Hero.** “From the placement schedule to the reviewed decision.”  
“Set up the approved mark, observe it in authorized recordings, compare the named feed with the agreement, and prepare the evidence for your commercial team.” CTA “See how Placement Audit works.” Secondary “Browse the four products.”

**Sections.**

1. **One event, one evidence trail.** “Follow the same opening replay from the approved Northline mark to a reviewed feed discrepancy.” Make the selected entity persist across the four steps.
2. **Four distinct responsibilities.** Show the four modules in sequence, with the operator, inputs and outputs from their respective pages.
3. **Fit the current process.** “Work from the schedule, authorized recordings, exposure output and annotations you already use. We’ll confirm format and access during discovery.”
4. **Where staff sign off.** “Operations approves measurement exports. A named reviewer confirms detections and interpretations. Commercial owns sponsor-facing action.”
5. **Start with the first paid module.** “Choose an event package and compare one Placement Audit with your reporting baseline.” CTA “Scope an evaluation.”

### Sponsor Assets · /platform/sponsor-assets

**Hero.** “Start with the artwork and placements you approved.”  
“Keep sponsor identity, permitted mark variants and the placement schedule in one reviewable reference.” CTA “See the example asset library.”

**Sections.**

1. **Approve a mark before it enters measurement.** “Record artwork version, placement type, eligible feeds and permission status.” Show light and dark variants and a pending version with an owner.
2. **Attach the requirement.** “Link the sponsor and event package to the named feed, position and placement rule in the approved schedule.” Show term S-04 and the corresponding mark.
3. **Leave a version trail.** “Give the measurement operations lead the owner, effective date and approved reference used for each comparison.”
4. **Pass approved inputs to measurement.** “Review the intended file formats and artwork rights during pilot scoping.” CTA “See Exposure Analytics.”

### Exposure Analytics · /platform/exposure-analytics

**Hero.** “See when and where an approved mark appears.”  
“Compare recorded feeds by on-screen position, duration and visible portion. Open the source behind each estimate.” CTA “Compare the replay feeds.”

**Sections.**

2. **Inspect a detection.** “Read the source ID, version, match window and coverage. A reviewer can correct uncertain results.”
3. **Show coverage gaps explicitly.** “Missing footage stays outside the observed window. An unreadable mark goes to review.”
4. **Carry observations into placement review.** “Use authorized production recordings or approved outputs agreed during discovery.” CTA “See Placement Audit.”

### Placement Audit · /platform/placement-audit

“Compare the feed, replay and overlay specified in the agreement with the clips and measurements you can inspect.” CTA “Open the Placement Audit demo.” Secondary “Request a paid pilot.”

3. **Keep commercial action visible.** “A confirmed discrepancy can be routed for make-good review. A commercial owner chooses the next action.”
4. **Compare against the current process.** “Run a paid evaluation against your exposure dashboard, schedule, manual annotations and reconciled reporting.” CTA “Scope a paid Placement Audit.”

### Sponsor Report · /platform/sponsor-report

**Hero.** “Give commercial the evidence behind the recap.”  
“Prepare a reviewable report with the agreed placement, supporting clip, measurement rule and decision history.” CTA “View the example record.”

**Sections.**

## 5. Copy and page metadata rules

- Use the live names Sponsor Assets, Exposure Analytics, Placement Audit and Sponsor Report verbatim across nav, screenshot labels, case workbench and exports.
- Lead with the named feed, event and placement decision. Use “observed,” “suggested,” “reviewed” and “approved for export” only for the corresponding states.
- Add “DEMO · SIMULATED DATA” to every preview route, source panel, case record, fixture screenshot and export. Label the product suite and plan as proposed in commercial copy.
- Homepage title “SponsorEvidence | Sports Sponsorship Delivery Verification.” Product title template “[Module] | SponsorEvidence.” Use a unique description and original static open-graph art for each core page. Exclude demo CRM and sign-in from search indexing when live.
- Use original or properly permitted footage, illustrations, UI, icons and copy. No stock mockups of existing analytics software, borrowed league marks, template platform branding or default favicon.

### Methodology and Sources

**“What an exposure observation contains.”** “A source reference and time window identify the recording. The observation estimates position, visible duration and visible portion for an approved mark. The placement requirement specifies which feed, event and position to check.”  
**“How we handle missing coverage.”** “If the supplied recording ends before the placement or leaves part of the required window unobserved, the review remains open. The case records the missing window and who can help obtain it.”  
**“Read the market context.”** “Shikenso describes AI-assisted sports sponsorship analytics, including on-screen position and duration in its logo detection methodology.
