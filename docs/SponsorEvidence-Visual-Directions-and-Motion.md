# SponsorEvidence visual directions and motion

SponsorEvidence follows one fictional opening replay from stadium to report. The main broadcast shows the Northline mark. The official vertical feed is the required placement under term S-04, and the mark is absent from the inspected replay. The same image, timecode and source IDs recur as the site moves from photography to comparison graphics to the reviewer’s workbench. This lets visitors recognize the evidence before they learn how to operate the product.

Use a new image, diagram, table, or product view for each idea the visitor needs to understand. Reserve cards for individual records, offers, use cases and source documents. Frame geometry stays flat across all three directions. Motion comes from cuts, masks, linear translation, tracking lines, time alignment and flat scale changes. Source and report examples carry **DEMO · SIMULATED DATA**.

## Three visual directions

### Matchday Replay

**Story.** A floodlit goal fills the first scene. The picture holds as a flat vertical cut appears at its edge. A shared time ruler makes it clear the two frames depict the same moment. The site cuts from the scale of the stadium to the evidence inside a single frame.

**Design.** Warm paper #F7F5ED, deep pitch green #184B3A, charcoal #131816 and lime #C5E67A for observed visibility on dark surfaces. Instrument Serif for editorial headlines and Geist for body and product typography. Use art-directed match photography, viewfinder brackets, full-bleed scenes, contact prints and light sections with generous captions. The workbench opens in charcoal with one bright signed clause.

**Signature motion.** The hero H1 rises through line masks. The 16:9 scene opens from the left and the 9:16 view enters from the right. Scrolling aligns their playheads, brings in the clause from above and reveals the documented discrepancy. Match-cut the 00:18:42 marker into the timeline below.

**Later scenes.** A pitch schematic supports the permissions explanation, a product card sequence borrows the rhythm of a contact sheet, and the closing CTA pairs “Bring one disputed placement” with a final strip of the replay. A dark-green footer carries highlighted pitch markings in its art band.

### Broadcast Control Room

**Story.** The buyer enters the review console in the hero. Two flat sources, a shared timecode and a single tracking rectangle explain the product immediately. Scrolling expands the time ruler, exposes coverage and makes room for the signed clause and case decision.

**Design.** Midnight graphite #101614, moss #29372E, pale ink #EEF3ED and citron #D5EC86 with amber reserved for incomplete coverage. Barlow Semi Condensed for broadcast-size headings and Geist with tabular numerals for the interface. Use footage plates, timecode typography, marker lines, a crisp feed-routing diagram and restrained status chips. Break the dark canvas with a white evidence sheet and a light pricing chapter.

**Signature motion.** The two feeds open with horizontal masks. A playhead travels right and brings the mark rectangle into view. The signed term drops into the frame from above. Source rows appear with a short bottom-up stagger. A flat cut into the light reviewer pane makes the human action the next beat.

**Later scenes.** The four modules are a horizontal strip of monitor-ready panels. The use-case visual changes from analyst console to measurement brief to a routing diagram. The pilot CTA is a small operator handoff card plus a large clear button. The footer echoes the timecode baseline.

### Evidence Atlas

**Story.** Start with a top-down stadium and three labeled feed paths. Follow the selected path into two source stills and the signed clause. The last image is a report exhibit with a visible chain of custody through source, review and export.

**Design.** Bone #F6F2E7, ink navy #132431, pine #23382C, cobalt #254ACB for traced paths and clay #A54434 for evidence gaps. Gloock editorial serif and Geist UI. Use flat cartographic linework, print grain, plot labels and calibrated type. Build the workbench on an eggshell base with a navy source shelf and cobalt focus accents.

**Signature motion.** The stadium plan reveals downward through a mask. Feed routes draw in one at a time. Scroll follows the selected line into the first source card from the right. The contract enters from the left and its connector lines end beside the evidence and decision. The report exhibit later unfolds upward as a flat paper layer.

**Later scenes.** Stack a cutaway venue illustration, path map, diagram and annotated document in distinct chapters. Product card faces read as evidence exhibits. Close with the complete simplified contract-to-report trace beside the pilot invitation, then a bone-and-ink footer.

## Motion rules for implementation

- Use one Lenis instance on public marketing pages, with its scroll events updating ScrollTrigger and its RAF driven by the GSAP ticker. Keep a single animation clock and dispose listeners, ticker and triggers on route changes. Prefer native scroll in the demo, form and nested scroll areas. [Lenis GSAP integration](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis#gsap-scrolltrigger)
- Use ScrollTrigger for chapter progress, one bounded horizontal suite and one short pinned replay comparison on eligible desktop layouts. Separate reveals from scrubbed timelines. Use roughly 380–650 ms for entrances, 40–75 ms between related rows and 120–200 ms for control feedback. Start with modest distances and test a brisk wheel scroll, touch swipe, keyboard jump and browser back motion. [GSAP ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/)
- Use a consistent direction for each type of reveal. Left and right reveal distinct media sources. Upward reveals introduce a title, card or conclusion. Downward reveals extract a term, ruler or source trail. Preserve normal reading order under all transforms. No element tilts, skews or rotates.
- Keep one accessible heading and reveal visual line wrappers around it. Recalculate splits when font or width changes. In reduced-motion mode use native scroll, complete stills and ordinary vertical content. Keep code-free initial states readable if script initialization fails. [GSAP SplitText](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/SplitText/) · [GSAP matchMedia](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/gsap.matchMedia%28%29/) · [W3C reduced motion](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C39.html)
- Start with lightweight hero posters. Defer video and charts outside the opening viewport, pause when offscreen and offer a motion toggle. Use frame-accurate scrub only after testing media delivery and seek behavior. Keep the important frame and source labels visible without video.
- Test the hero, chapter rail, horizontal suite, CTA, dashboard, and footer at 1440 px and 390 px. Check Tab focus in pinned sections, anchor/back/forward behavior, keyboard access to replay and tables, visible labels, meaningful still states, and button contrast. Studio mockups illustrate direction. Final UI text, source details, measurements and interactions come from the website and audit specifications.

**Recommended direction.** Matchday Replay has the clearest opening for a rights-holder buyer. The first scroll turns an instantly recognizable match moment into source-linked evidence. Broadcast Control Room is useful if the dashboard should lead the identity. Evidence Atlas is the strongest choice if diagrams and source maps should lead the brand.
